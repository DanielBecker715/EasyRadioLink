EasyRadioLink 1.3.0: **voices that sound like a real radio link**. A new *Distance (weak signal)* setting makes the
other stations sound far away - fading that swirls through the voice, static that breathes, a narrower, harsher voice -
and the squelch tail is now off by default. And like on a real radio, **only one station can talk on a frequency at a
time**.

## New

- **Distance (weak signal)** (Settings > Radio Sounds, 0 - 100 %, default 35 %): the stations you receive sound far
  away, like a long-distance or field radio link. Multipath lets notches swim through the voice (the watery, swirling
  sound of distant radio), the signal fades and the static comes up and breathes with the receiver's automatic gain
  control, and the voice gets narrower and harsher, like a military handset. The band sets the character: slow, deep
  fading and the strongest swirl below 30 MHz (HF, CB, MW), above 50 % with a faint heterodyne whistle; fast flutter
  on AM above 30 MHz (AIR, UHF AM); on FM the voice keeps its level while a bright hiss surges up whenever the signal
  fades below the FM threshold; on the DIG band no static or fading but the gritty sound of CVSD "secure voice"
  coding. No crackle, no dropouts - nothing clicks or cuts out. Voice to static is about 24 dB at 35 % (clearly far
  away, easy to understand), 14 dB at 70 % and 7 dB at 100 % (the voice swims in the static but stays
  understandable). It acts on the whole received voice at any radio effect strength (also on the clean share of that
  mix); it is not used on clean frequencies or when the radio effect strength is 0 %, and the squelch tail and the
  start / end sounds stay clean. The **Audio Preview** on the Radio tab uses it too, so you can tune it while you hear
  yourself.
- **Profiles up front, with a ready-made Helicopter profile**: the profile choice (with *New*, *Copy*, *Rename*,
  *Delete*) is now at the top of the Settings tab instead of under Advanced settings, and the profile you choose stays
  active after a restart. Every settings folder gets a ready-made **Helicopter** profile once: helicopter background
  sound at 40 %, *Boost my voice* 40 %, radio effect strength 20 %, distance 15 %, with the key bindings of your
  default profile. Delete or rename it as you like - it does not come back; an existing profile with that name is left
  alone.
- **Boost my voice** (Settings > Radio Sounds, 0 - 100 %, default 0 %): makes your voice louder for everybody who
  hears you, so it stands out more from your background sound and the static (+10 dB at 100 %; loud peaks are rounded
  off smoothly instead of clipping). Everybody normalises the voices they receive, so it is sent with your background
  sound and applied by the listeners (not on clean frequencies). With 50 % the voice is about 4 dB further above a
  helicopter at 50 % while you talk, with 100 % about 7 - 9 dB. Listeners with 1.1 / 1.2 or on a 1.1 / 1.2 server hear
  you without it (no protocol change).
- **Voice activation (VOX) defaults**: the *Voice level threshold* starts at 60 % (-38.4 dBFS, before -59 dBFS: less
  background noise starts a transmission) and is now shown in percent like the other sliders (0 % = -96 dBFS,
  100 % = 0 dBFS); the *minimum transmission length* starts at 700 ms (before 300 ms), so short words are not cut
  off. Settings you already saved keep their values.
- **Squelch tail off by default**: new profiles start without the "kssht" at the end of a transmission. Profiles that
  already have the setting keep it - switch it off under Settings > Radio Sounds > *Squelch tail*.
- **One speaker per frequency** (busy channel lockout, on by default): while somebody is talking on a frequency,
  nobody else can transmit on it - like real radios with busy channel lockout. The server decides: the first station
  to transmit holds the frequency until 0.3 s after its last transmission; voice of other stations on that frequency
  is not forwarded to anybody. On the radio a red **BUSY** is lit while another station uses your frequency. If you
  press push-to-talk then, you hear a short busy tone (two low beeps, only you hear it, played over the received voice
  without delaying it), BUSY flashes and nothing is sent for the whole press: release and press again once the
  frequency is free. If two stations press at the same moment, the first one gets through and the other one hears
  the busy tone and stops transmitting. With voice activation (VOX) nothing is sent while the frequency is busy (busy
  tone at most once per second). A press that sends nothing does not block your receiver on half-duplex servers.
  Radio check (echo) frequencies follow the same rule; your own echo never makes the frequency busy.
- Server: new setting *One speaker per frequency (busy channel lockout)* in the *Radio realism* group of the server
  window, `--busy-lockout` for the command-line server (`BUSY_CHANNEL_LOCKOUT` in `server.cfg`, default `true`). A
  change applies at once. **Server Info** in the client shows whether it is on.

## Fixed

- **Background sounds were hard to hear** (jet, prop, helicopter): they were mixed in far below the voice and were
  buried in the static, on HF almost completely (a helicopter at 40 % was only about 1 dB above the static). Their
  level is now set by their loudness in the radio band, so every sound is equally audible through a radio: about 9 dB
  below the voice at 40 % volume (the voice stays on top) and clearly above the static, also on HF and with distance.
  100 % volume is about as loud as the other radio sounds, and lower volumes drop less steeply than before
  (40 % = -8 dB, 25 % = -12 dB; before -13 / -20 dB). The deep rumble below 150 Hz is removed, like behind a
  headset microphone - a radio does not pass it anyway, and at a lower radio effect strength it no longer drones
  louder than the voice. Every listener hears this (the level is set on the receiving side), with any 1.1 / 1.2 /
  1.3 sender.

## Notes

- One speaker per frequency needs no protocol change: 1.3 servers and clients work with 1.1 / 1.2 ones. A 1.1 / 1.2
  client on a 1.3 server is locked out like everybody else, but it shows no BUSY and plays no busy tone - it just
  isn't heard while the frequency is busy. A 1.3 client on an older server shows the setting as off and transmits as
  before (older servers lock nothing out). To keep the old behaviour (everybody may talk at the same time, e.g. with
  *Radio interference*), switch the setting off on the server.
- The distance is applied by each listener, like the other radio effects: everybody hears the others with their
  own setting. Recordings of received audio contain it as well; your own voice (mic output device, recording
  of your own transmissions) does not.
